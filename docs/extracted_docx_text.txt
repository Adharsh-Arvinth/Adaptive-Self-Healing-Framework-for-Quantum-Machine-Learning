An Adaptive Self-Healing Framework for Quantum Machine LearningAbstractVariational Quantum Circuits (VQCs), the trainable core of most near-term Quantum Machine Learning (QML) models, are notoriously fragile to train, with barren plateaus, poorly chosen learning rates, and unstable optimizer settings routinely derailing training runs. Existing responses are either manual and expert-dependent, relying on hand-tuned hyperparameter schedules, or automated but non-adaptive, such as one-shot AutoML search or fixed rule-based repair; none treat repair selection itself as a decision problem that improves from accumulated outcomes. This paper proposes an Adaptive Self-Healing Framework for QML that closes a continuous monitor-diagnose-repair-verify loop around VQC training. A health-monitoring system extracts training telemetry, a rule-based detector coupled with a confidence-gated diagnosis engine identifies the likely failure mode, and a six-arm repair library encodes candidate fixes as pure configuration transforms. As the central contribution, repair selection is formalized as a contextual Thompson Sampling bandit with per-arm Bayesian linear regression posteriors, updated online from a multi-objective reward signal, with a digital-twin proxy trainer vetting each repair before deployment. A complete vertical slice, implemented in PennyLane, has been validated end-to-end across all fourteen architectural phases for a representative failure path, ahead of a planned ablation study comparing random, greedy, rule-based, and adaptive policies to quantify gains in recovery sample efficiency.Literature SurveyS.noAuthor(s) / YearTitle / FocusMethodologyKey FindingsResearch Gap1McClean et al. To formalize a repair library and a safe pre-deployment verification pipeline, consisting of at least six candidate repair actions expressed as pure, side-effect-free configuration transforms (e.g., learning-rate reduction, optimizer substitution, gradient clipping, re-initialization, ansatz simplification, batch-size adjustment), combined with an oscillation/safety filter and a short-horizon digital-twin proxy trainer that vet each candidate repair before it is committed to the live training run. To formalize repair-strategy selection as a contextual Thompson Sampling bandit problem, implementing per-arm Bayesian linear regression posteriors over a shared context vector derived from diagnosis output and training telemetry, a multi-objective reward fusion function combining loss improvement, gradient-health recovery, training-time cost, and stability, and dual online learning through live posterior updates and persistent knowledge-base storage — positioning this adaptive bandit formulation as the central novelty distinguishing the framework from static, rule-based repair. To implement safe, reversible deployment of repairs with quantitative learning evaluation, including rollback of repairs that underperform their digital-twin prediction and instrumentation of cumulative regret relative to an oracle policy, so that the efficiency and reliability of the adaptive repair policy can be measured objectively over time. To empirically validate the complete framework and document it to academic research standards, by demonstrating all fourteen architectural phases end-to-end on a concrete VQC classification task for at least one full failure path, conducting a statistically meaningful ablation study (following QNode vectorization) comparing random, greedy, rule-based, and adaptive repair policies, and producing a formal Software Requirements Specification and supporting documentation suitable for thesis or publication-track review.
